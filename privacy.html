Right to Reply privacy policy

Effective 24 November 2026.

Right to Reply is an independent browser extension that displays publicly reviewed political party responses next to relevant phrases in online articles. It is free, will remain free and will never contain advertising. The extension does not require an account.

Local processing of website content

The extension reads visible text in web pages on which it is enabled, to identify participating parties and relevant keywords. It uses the current website hostname to apply per-site settings. This processing takes place on your device. Article text, page URLs, browsing history, keyword matches, political preferences and reply-reading activity are not transmitted to us, political parties or our data provider. No browsing or reply-reading history is kept. The extension does not read form input values or request access to your browser history.

What is stored on your device?

The extension stores its enabled/disabled setting, hostnames and preferences for sites you explicitly configure, complete public response datasets and refresh metadata in browser-local extension storage. These settings are not synchronised to our servers. Public datasets are refreshed while the browser permits background execution; a failed refresh may use the last valid dataset for up to 24 hours. Uninstalling the extension removes its local extension storage. Disabling the extension stops article matching but does not necessarily stop background dataset refreshes.

Network requests and third parties

The Chrome package retrieves the public source directory, party metadata, response bundles and the project owner's optional disclaimer from GitHub's raw content service (raw.githubusercontent.com) over HTTPS. It requests the complete approved datasets on installation, when due at browser startup, on a daily background schedule or when you explicitly select refresh. All readers request the same public datasets, independently of the pages they read. Requests omit credentials and referrers. GitHub may receive ordinary connection metadata, including your IP address and request time, under its own privacy statement. Political parties do not receive reader information from the extension. If you explicitly open an optional project-owner disclaimer link, the destination receives the normal information associated with a website visit and its own privacy practices apply.

Permissions

Website access is used to recognise phrases and display replies on ordinary HTTP and HTTPS pages. Local storage is needed for public datasets and settings; alarms are needed for scheduled updates. Temporary active-tab access when you open the toolbar popup allows the extension to show and change the setting for that website without requesting the broader tabs permission.

No tracking, advertising or sale of data

There is no telemetry, analytics, advertising, user profiling, sale of personal information or sharing of reader activity. The extension downloads data, not executable code. Information accessed by the extension is used solely for the disclosed user-facing purpose. The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Control and contact

Use the toolbar popup to disable matching globally or for the current site. You can also restrict website access through your browser's extension settings or uninstall the extension. For privacy questions, corrections or concerns, use the developer contact provided on the Chrome Web Store listing. Do not post private information in public project discussions.

Material changes to these practices will be disclosed before introducing them. This page applies to the extension; websites you visit, including the demonstration site and GitHub, have their own hosting and privacy arrangements.
