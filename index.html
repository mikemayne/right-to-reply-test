See Right to Reply in action

Right to Reply is a browser extension that gives participating political parties an opportunity to respond to specific keywords when they are mentioned in an article. It does not decide whether an article is accurate or misleading. It simply makes the party's approved response available to the reader.

See how the popup behaves when several parties reply to the same phrase. Hover, click or tap an underline. Only the short responses appear initially. Select a short statement to reveal its party name and longer response. All underlines and short statements have the same neutral styling, regardless of party. Statements from participating parties are displayed in a randomly determined order, and no party receives preferential treatment. Each response can be expanded or collapsed independently.

All five parties and their responses are invented for this interface test. No extension or external party repositories are needed. The newspaper navigation is decorative.

Privacy by design

Right to Reply does not collect or transmit your browsing history, article content, political preferences or which responses you read. All matching and reply lookups take place on your device. No accounts, advertising, analytics or tracking.

The extension downloads the same complete set of approved responses for all participating parties on a daily background schedule, not in response to your browsing. GitHub or the configured download provider may receive ordinary connection information such as your IP address, but the extension does not send your reading activity. External websites you choose to visit have their own privacy practices.

Because Right to Reply adds underlines and popups to webpages, a website may be able to detect that the extension is installed and observe when a response popup is opened. Right to Reply itself does not record or transmit this activity. It cannot prevent websites from tracking interactions with their own pages.

All extension source code is publicly visible on GitHub. Feel free to ask ChatGPT to inspect the code for evidence of tracking or the collection of personal data.
