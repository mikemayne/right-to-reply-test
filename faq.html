For political parties and readers

Frequently asked questions

Right to Reply gives UK political parties a way to put their own positions in front of readers when the party and a relevant keyword or phrase appear in an article. It is a right of reply, not an endorsement.

What can a party say?

All verification takes place in public. Statements must be either factual claims supported by verified, credible sources or sincerely held opinions, clearly expressed as such. Neither may use manipulative techniques described in Defeating Manipulation.

For example, “Brexit means Brexit” is out: it is a slogan that does not explain a position. “We think Brexit is better for Britain” is in: it expresses a clear political opinion. The same rules apply to the opposing view. Any factual claims used to support an opinion still require evidence.

Names of individuals are forbidden as response keywords. Personal allegations and personal attacks are forbidden, as are responses to direct quotes from opposing parties. Parties may respond to policy topics and political positions, but not to specific quotations from their opponents. Each entry consists of a one-line response of up to 128 characters and an extended description of up to 255 characters.

Is it free and politically neutral?

Yes. Right to Reply is free for all officially registered UK political parties and all readers, will remain free, will never include advertising and will apply the same rules impartially to every party. Participation is entirely opt-in, and the offer remains open to every eligible party. No party receives preferential placement.

How do you ensure accountability?

All communication about proposed content, supporting evidence, objections, corrections and editorial decisions takes place in public GitHub pull requests (PRs). There are no private content negotiations or off-record editorial decisions. That public record is intended to keep everyone honest, including me.

Why are there no external links in replies?

External content can change after a response has been approved. Published replies are therefore plain text only, with no external links, redirects or executable content. Links to supporting evidence belong in the public PR, where they can be checked during review.

Do parties need technical expertise?

No. The technical bar to entry is deliberately low. Each party nominates one authorised GitHub account. With a write-capable ChatGPT connection to GitHub, its representative can describe changes in ordinary English, check the proposed wording and submit a public PR. Direct editing through GitHub is also possible without coding or paid AI tools.

Read the ChatGPT setup and update guide.

Who has the final say?

I do. It is my plugin, and I retain final editorial control. Decisions must follow the same published rules regardless of party or political position. Rejections will identify the rule at issue, and parties can challenge decisions in the public PR discussion. The decision and its reasoning will be public.

How long will approval take?

No review or response time is guaranteed. Accuracy, honesty, cooperation and respectful conduct matter; political affiliation and agreement with my own views do not. Abusive conduct will be flagged.

What happens if facts change?

Content may be removed without warning if facts change, new evidence contradicts its supporting evidence, or the material presents a potential legal risk. The reason will be recorded publicly to the extent lawful and appropriate. Parties can submit corrected wording and evidence through a new public PR.

What about reader privacy?

Matching and reply lookups take place on the reader's device. The extension downloads the same complete approved response bundle for all readers on a background schedule; it does not send browsing history, article content, political preferences or reply-reading activity. There is no advertising, analytics or tracking.
